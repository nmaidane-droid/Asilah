Discovering Asilah

Asilah, a jewel of Morocco's Atlantic coast, invites you on an enchanting journey through time and beauty. History, art and culture come together in its narrow lanes, its imposing walls and its picturesque shoreline. Let yourself be charmed by the singular atmosphere of Asilah, where the past meets the present to create an unforgettable experience.

Key facts

Location and climate

Location

On Morocco's Atlantic coast, Asilah sits between Tangier and Larache. Its position close to the Strait of Gibraltar gives it a temperate climate, shaped by both Mediterranean and Atlantic influences.

Climate

Asilah's climate is mild and pleasant all year round. Summers are warm and sunny, averaging around 25 °C. Winters are mild and damp, averaging around 15 °C.

Beaches

Asilah's beaches are known for their fine sand and clear water. The coast offers a variety of coves and bays, ideal for swimming, sunbathing and water sports.

Twenty centuries

A brief history of the town

Antiquity

The Roman era

Founded by the Romans under the name “Zilil”, Asilah was an important trading port. Remains of fortifications and baths are still visible today.

Middle Ages

Almoravids and Almohads

These dynasties built the ramparts and fortifications that protected the town from invaders.

16th century

The Portuguese era

The Portuguese take control of Asilah. They build a fort and use the town as a base for their trading operations.

Since 1956

The modern era

After Morocco's independence, Asilah enjoyed a revival and became a significant destination, drawing visitors from around the world.

Crafts and cultural heritage

Traditional crafts

Asilah is known for pottery, embroidery, basketry and jewellery. Workshops and shops are found throughout the medina.

Cultural heritage

The Kasbah mosque, the museum of contemporary art and the Ibn Khaldoun library bear witness to the town's rich history.

The Asilah Festival

A major cultural event, every July and August. It draws artists from around the world — painting, sculpture, music and dance.

Food

A blend of Mediterranean and Moroccan flavours: fresh fish, seafood, vegetables and spices in the local restaurants.

The medina

Fortifications

The magnificent walls and gates of the medina

The ramparts

Built in the 16th century, they encircle the medina and offer sweeping views over the town and the coast. These imposing walls speak of Asilah's fortified past.

The gates

Carved and decorated with arabesque motifs, they are fine examples of Moorish architecture. Each has its own history and its own symbolic name.

Walks

Walking along the ramparts and the gates is an immersion in the history and the singular atmosphere of the town.

By the water

Beaches and walks along the shore

Swimming

Asilah's beaches are ideal for swimming and relaxing. The water is generally calm and clean.

Walking

A walk along the shore opens up sweeping views of the ocean, the cliffs and the beaches, in fresh air and the sound of the waves.

Water sports

Ideal conditions for surfing, windsurfing and kitesurfing. Schools and rental centres are available locally.

Fishing

Local fishermen run trips out to sea. The fresh catch can then be enjoyed in the town's restaurants.

All year round

Festivals and cultural events

The Asilah Festival

A major cultural event held every July and August. Painting, sculpture, music and dance, by artists from around the world.

The Sea Festival

Held every July, it celebrates Asilah's maritime culture: parades, concerts and boat races.

Music evenings

All year round, in cafés, restaurants and public squares. Local and international groups, from jazz to flamenco.

Craft markets

A chance to meet the artisans and to find genuine rugs, jewellery, pottery and leatherwork.
